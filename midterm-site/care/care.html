The Essentials of Cat Care 🐾

Small daily habits make a big difference.

🥣

Food & Water

Give your cat balanced nutrition and clean drinking water.

  • Choose complete cat food suited to their life stage.
  • Keep fresh water available at all times.
  • Follow appropriate portions and feeding guidance.
  • Avoid sudden diet changes unless advised by a vet.
🪮

Grooming

Regular grooming helps maintain a comfortable coat.

  • Brush regularly, especially long-haired cats.
  • Check for tangles and unusual skin changes.
  • Trim claws carefully when needed.
  • Introduce grooming gradually and gently.
🩺

Veterinary Care

Preventive care helps your cat stay healthy.

  • Arrange regular veterinary checkups.
  • Keep vaccinations and parasite prevention current.
  • Monitor appetite, behavior, and litter-box habits.
  • Contact a vet about concerning changes.
🧶

Exercise & Play

Play provides physical activity and mental stimulation.

  • Use toys that encourage chasing and pouncing.
  • Provide scratching posts and climbing spaces.
  • Offer several short play sessions.
  • Rotate toys to keep things interesting.
🏠

A Safe Home

Create a calm environment where your cat can relax.

  • Provide a quiet resting place and hiding spots.
  • Secure windows and balconies.
  • Keep toxic plants and household chemicals away.
  • Give your cat access to a clean litter box.
💛

Love & Attention

Build trust while respecting your cat's boundaries.

  • Let your cat approach you voluntarily.
  • Learn their body language.
  • Provide companionship and enrichment.
  • Respect their need for personal space.

Keeping Your Cat Safe ⚠️

Knowing what to avoid is just as important as knowing what to do.

Important Safety Tips

  • Dangerous foods: Keep chocolate, onions, garlic, alcohol, and other potentially toxic foods away from cats.
  • Plants: Lilies are extremely dangerous to cats. All parts, including pollen, can cause severe poisoning. Contact a veterinarian immediately if exposure occurs.
  • Human medication: Never give human medicines to your cat unless a veterinarian specifically instructs you to.
  • Outdoor hazards: Secure windows and balconies, and consider safe indoor enrichment or supervised outdoor access.
  • Emergency signs: Difficulty breathing, collapse, suspected poisoning, or straining without passing urine requires urgent veterinary attention.
